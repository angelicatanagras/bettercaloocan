# City Projects

Ongoing and past infrastructure and public works projects affecting Caloocan, based on public reporting and official issuances. Caloocan does not currently publish a single, searchable projects dashboard, so this page draws on independently verifiable news and government sources rather than an official master list.

---

## Flooding Is the City's Central Infrastructure Challenge

Caloocan sits low and adjacent to the KAMANAVA area (Kalookan–Malabon–Navotas–Valenzuela), which has struggled with flooding for decades. In **July 2025, the city was placed under a state of calamity** after severe floods affected an estimated 50,000 residents.

---

## KAMANAVA Flood Control Project (National, DPWH-led)

- Conceptualized in 1997 after Malacañang's First Flood Summit, with an original ₱3.097-billion budget covering floodgates, dikes, pumping stations, and drainage improvements across Caloocan, Malabon, Navotas, and Valenzuela.
- Originally scheduled for completion in 2007 (84 months), later extended to 2009 — but the project remains incompletely effective decades later, with structural and maintenance issues persisting.
- Placed under the operational control of the **Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA)** by Executive Order No. 598, with the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) retaining administrative supervision.
- This is a **national government project**, not a Caloocan City-funded one — the city government's role is largely coordination, local drainage maintenance, and easement clearing.

---

## City-Funded Local Works

- **Libis Espina road rehabilitation and drainage project** — a ₱9.6-million project scheduled to run March–August 2025. Residents and vendors in the area have publicly reported that flooding persisted after completion, raising questions about the project's effectiveness. Included here because it's a real, dated, city-level project with both a verified cost and a verified (mixed) outcome — not because it's a success story.
- Beyond this project, no current, itemized, verified list of the City Engineering Office's active infrastructure projects was found in public reporting. The city does not appear to publish a projects monitoring page equivalent to DPWH's national project portals.
