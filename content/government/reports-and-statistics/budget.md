# Budget & City Finances

How the City Government of Caloocan raises and spends public money, based on figures reported in the city's approved annual budget and national audit/allocation records.

---

## FY2025 Annual Budget

The Sangguniang Panlungsod (City Council) passed Caloocan's **FY2025 Annual Budget at ₱10.756 billion**, approved on three separate readings as required by the Local Government Code, and formally transmitted by Vice Mayor Karina Teh to Mayor Along Malapitan on October 17, 2024.

**Top-funded offices for FY2025:**

| Office / Department                             | FY2025 Allocation |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| City Health Department                          | ₱1.15 billion     |
| City Environmental Management Department (CEMD) | ₱1.11 billion     |
| Caloocan City Medical Center                    | ₱746.8 million    |
| Caloocan City North Medical Center (Camarin)    | ₱442.9 million    |
| Office of the City Mayor                        | ₱445 million      |
| University of Caloocan City (UCC)               | ₱309.3 million    |
| Office of the Vice Mayor                        | ₱30.7 million     |

Health and environmental management consistently rank as the two largest line items — consistent with the city's ongoing waste-management and flood-prone-area challenges (see [Waste Segregation](/services/garbage-waste-disposal/learn-proper-waste-segregation-and-disposal-methods) and [City Projects](/government/reports-and-statistics/projects) below).

## Where the Money Comes From

Most of an LGU's budget in the Philippines comes from two sources:

1. **Local revenue** — real property tax, business taxes (see [Renew Permits & Pay Local Business Taxes](/services/business/renew-permits-and-pay-local-business-taxes)), fees, and charges collected directly by the city.
2. **National Tax Allotment (NTA)** — Caloocan's share of national internal revenue collections, transferred annually by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) under the Mandanas-Garcia ruling. The exact peso figure of Caloocan's individual NTA share is published in DBM's annual Local Budget Memorandum, but a specific verified per-city figure for Caloocan was not available from public reporting at the time of writing — check the [DBM National Tax Allotment page](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/national-tax-allotment-nta) directly for the current year's LGU-level breakdown.

## Independent Audit

Local government spending is independently audited every year by the **Commission on Audit (COA)**, which publishes an Annual Audit Report (AAR) per LGU. Caloocan's AARs are published at [coa.gov.ph](https://www.coa.gov.ph) (search "Caloocan City Annual Audit Report"); the site does not allow automated access, so reports must be opened directly in a browser.

One notable, independently verifiable COA finding: in August 2023, the **COA en banc upheld the disallowance of ₱198 million** in confidential and intelligence funds spent by the Caloocan City government from 2008–2013, after finding the liquidation documents submitted were inadequate and that the city had no active peace-and-order program for part of that period.
